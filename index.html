A bit more about me

I came to geospatial research through my curiosity about cities.

I studied Spatial Planning at Warsaw University of Technology, where I became interested in how we represent, analyse and understand the places we live in. Over time, I found myself increasingly drawn to the technical side of that question.

GIS opened up new ways of looking at space. BIM introduced me to the level of detail behind the built environment. My bachelor's thesis on City Information Modeling brought those interests together, exploring how digital representations of cities can support spatial planning.

Since then, I've been exploring 3D spatial data, geospatial analysis and reality-based modelling, including research work at KU Leuven. I'm interested in the point where spatial questions meet computational methods — and in learning how to investigate that intersection properly.

I'm still learning, experimenting and figuring out which questions I want to spend the next few years pursuing. That's part of the point. I want to build a career around research without losing the curiosity that got me here.

Research internship KU Leuven
Leuven, Belgium

Exploring 3D reconstruction methods

Geomatics Research Group

During my research internship, I explored workflows for 3D reconstruction and compared approaches involving COLMAP, GLOMAP, GlueMap and VGGT. My work focused on understanding the differences between methods, examining reconstruction accuracy and comparing point-cloud outputs.

The experience introduced me to new questions around 3D reconstruction, spatial representation and the possibilities of working with reality-based data.

Beyond the technical side

Curious by nature.
A researcher in progress.

I don't want my interest in technical methods to make me forget why I started looking at cities in the first place. Places are not just geometries, datasets or models. They are environments people experience, change and give meaning to.

That's why I like moving between different scales and ways of thinking — from the broader questions of spatial planning to the details of a point cloud, from reading research to trying something out for myself.

I'm building my skills step by step, and I'm excited by how much there still is to learn. For me, an academic path is not about having every answer already. It's about finding questions worth staying curious about.
